# Research grant funding for the pilot

Can we get grant money for trying teaching techniques with children and recording the data?
**Yes — and the pilot is shaped almost exactly like what the strongest funders pay for.** The
prevention thesis in [`ese-program.md`](ese-program.md) §7 — *does early supported inclusion
prevent a percentage of acquired behavior problems?* — is a genuine, unanswered research
question, and the ILP platform is the data-collection instrument no prior study has had. This
document maps who funds that kind of work, ranks the realistic paths, and flags what changed in
the 2025–26 federal funding environment. Compiled August 2026; verify open dates before filing
anything.

> **The shape that wins:** a **triad** — the ILP platform company + a university research
> partner (as principal investigator and IRB of record) + St. Lucie Public Schools (as the
> pilot site). That one structure lets the same pilot apply in three different funding lanes at
> once: small-business innovation money (company applies), education research money (university
> applies), and local child-outcome money (district or nonprofit applies). It also pairs
> directly with the university staffing pipeline in [`staffing-pipeline.md`](staffing-pipeline.md).

## 1. The ranked paths (best fit first)

### 1. ED/IES SBIR — the best near-term fit
The U.S. Department of Education's Small Business Innovation Research program funds **ed-tech
companies directly** to build and evaluate products in real classrooms — the district is the
pilot site, not the applicant.

- **Phase IA:** $250,000 / 9 months for a novel prototype. **Phase IB:** $250,000 to add a new
  component to an existing product (the ESE/AAC module on the existing ILP platform is a
  textbook Phase IB). **Phase II:** $1,000,000 / 2 years for full development **plus a
  school-based pilot evaluation** — exactly the per-student, per-module outcome data ILP
  generates by design.
- **Status: operating.** 2025 awards were made (explicitly including special-education
  technology); the annual cycle runs solicitation ~April, proposals ~June.
- **Needs:** a U.S. small business (<500 employees) as applicant, a district letter/site
  agreement, and an evaluation plan (university evaluator as subcontract).

### 2. Spencer Foundation Research-Practice Partnership grant — the best open foundation lane
Up to **$400K over 3 years**, purpose-built for a **district–university partnership studying a
locally defined problem**. The inclusion/behavior-prevention question is a textbook RPP research
agenda. Annual cycle (the 2026 deadline was July 10; plan for 2027). Pair it with a Spencer
Small Research Grant (**$50K**) for a pilot-year descriptive study. Needs an existing,
describable partnership and a university or 501(c)(3) fiscal home — another reason the
university partnership conversation comes first.

### 3. IES/NCSER special-education research grants — highest ceiling, highest uncertainty
The federal special-education research arm (NCSER, program 84.324A) is the single best
scientific home for the hypothesis: communication/AAC, behavior, autism, and inclusion are its
core topics, with **Development & Innovation awards ~$2M / 4 years** and **Initial Efficacy
~$3.8M / 5 years**. But IES was gutted in early 2025 (~$900M in contracts terminated,
competitions frozen) and is only now restarting: FY2027 grantmaking was announced August 2026
in three tranches, with the main research competitions expected in the **September–October 2026
announcements — not yet confirmed**. If 84.324A returns, a Development & Innovation application
(the supported-inclusion + AAC + adaptive-instruction model, with ILP as the delivery vehicle)
is the play. Hedge: the same design fits NIMH/NIDCD's R34 (≤$450K exploratory) → R01 pathway,
framed on behavior/communication outcomes — NIDCD+NIMH have specifically targeted minimally
verbal school-aged children with ASD in past initiatives, the closest historical federal match
to our thesis.

### 4. Fund the pilot year *now*: OAR + local St. Lucie money
- **Organization for Autism Research (OAR)** — Applied Research Competition, up to 9 awards of
  **$50,000**, explicitly for applied school/community intervention research. Small but fast,
  autism-specific, and prestige-relevant for later federal applications. Next letter-of-intent
  window ~March 2027.
- **Children's Services Council of St. Lucie County** — verified and real: an independent
  special taxing district funding child-outcome programs for 30,000+ St. Lucie children a year,
  with **School Readiness & School Success** among its priority areas, mandatory outcome
  measurement, and posted funding opportunities. Its accountability culture matches a data-rich
  pilot exactly. Awards are program-service dollars (tens to low hundreds of thousands), applied
  for by the district or a nonprofit fiscal agent — a natural home for the family-engagement
  and inclusion-support wraparound.
- **Community Foundation Martin–St. Lucie / Treasure Coast Giving Circle** ($10K–$50K strategic
  grants) and the **St. Lucie Education Foundation** — right-sized for AAC devices, materials,
  and family-engagement line items.

Together these can fund a Year-0 feasibility study that generates the preliminary data every
larger application above will demand.

### 5. The opportunistic second wave
- **OSEP "Stepping-Up Technology Implementation" (84.327S)** — ~$500K/yr for up to 4–5 years to
  implement **evidence-based** technology for students with disabilities in real schools. The
  ideal scale-up vehicle — but it funds *evidence-based* tools, so it sequences **after** the
  pilot produces evidence. A competition ran in 2025; the next cycle is unscheduled — watch
  Grants.gov. OSEP also periodically competes **model demonstration projects** (84.326M,
  ~$400–500K/yr, districts eligible as partners); none open at last check.
- **NSF STEM K-12 (solicitation 25-545)** — $350K–$750K awards, strong emphasis on **AI and
  emerging technologies in K-12 learning**. NSF archived its older K-12 education programs in
  2025 and consolidated into this one (roughly half the former budget, so competition is
  intense). Fits if the proposal foregrounds adaptive STEM learning with AI; the disability
  hypothesis rides along. NSF's own SBIR (Phase I $305K / Phase II $1.25M) is also operating,
  with learning technology a long-supported topic.

## 2. Bridge funding that requires no competition at all

Two statutory hooks let the district start the pilot from money it already has — worth knowing
because they make "we can begin without waiting on any grant" a true sentence in the pitch:

- **ESSA evidence tiers.** Title I/II/IV-A funds can pay for interventions at any of four
  evidence levels — including **Tier 4, "demonstrates a rationale,"** which *requires ongoing
  evaluation of effects*. A district can lawfully fund the ILP pilot from existing formula
  funds precisely by committing to the outcome-data collection the pilot is designed around.
  The evaluation obligation isn't a burden here; it's the product.
- **IDEA Part B voluntary CEIS.** Up to **15% of the district's IDEA allocation** may fund
  academic and behavioral supports for **not-yet-identified students in general education** — a
  direct statutory hook for "prevent the behavior problems from developing via early supported
  inclusion."

## 4. Mechanics that matter

- **Who applies where:** federal research grants (IES, NSF, NIH R-series) go to institutions
  with research infrastructure — in practice a **university as applicant/PI, district as
  partner site**. SBIR is the mirror image: the **company must apply**, with district and
  university as subawards. The triad structure covers both.
- **IRB and consent:** any funded research with children needs university IRB approval,
  **active parental consent** for research participation (distinct from routine instructional
  data under FERPA), district research-review approval, and assent procedures adapted to each
  child's communication ability — nonverbal students are a protected-plus population. Budget
  **3–6 months** for IRB plus district data-sharing agreements in any timeline.
- **The data double-edge:** ILP's per-student, per-setting behavior and mastery data is the
  pilot's greatest asset for grant reviewers *and* its heaviest compliance load (FERPA + IDEA
  confidentiality + human-subjects). The program's existing guardrails — no software-made
  decisions, audit trail, plain-language family view — are also its compliance story.

## 5. Uncertainty ledger (honesty first)

- Whether the main IES competitions (84.305A / 84.324A) return for FY2027 rides on the
  **September–October 2026 tranche announcements** — watch ies.ed.gov/funding and Grants.gov.
- OSEP's next Stepping-Up and model-demonstration cycles are unscheduled.
- NIH autism priorities have shifted toward etiology; the services-research lane is tighter.
- NSF's K-12 budget is roughly half its former size; competition is intense.
- All amounts and dates above are as published for the most recent cycles; **confirm the
  current solicitation before committing any date to a district or university partner.**

## 6. What this means for the pitch

The staffing question and the funding question answer each other. The university partnership
(see [`staffing-pipeline.md`](staffing-pipeline.md)) supplies the PI, the IRB, and the trained
hands in classrooms; the pilot's honest self-measurement — already a program commitment in
[`ese-program.md`](ese-program.md) §7 — is *literally the deliverable funders buy*. So the ask
to the superintendent is not "buy software." It is: **join a district + university + platform
pilot that outside money wants to fund, that can begin under ESSA Tier-4 / CEIS flexibility
you already have, and that answers a question no district has ever been able to measure.**
