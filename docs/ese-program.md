# The "Meet Them Where They Are" ESE Program

A doable, credible program a district can actually run — the operational form of
[`ese-applied-design.md`](ese-applied-design.md). Written to be read by an ESE director and
nodded at, not bristled at: it asks for a small start, keeps every legally-required human
decision human, and measures itself honestly.

> **The case, in full:** the nonverbal, the semi-verbal, and the just-plain-quiet child — no
> tantrums, no outbursts — should be **mainstreamed as soon as possible**. Because the behavior
> problems ESE spends years managing don't appear from nowhere: a percentage of them are **grown,
> out of frustration** — the frustration of not being heard, and the frustration of sitting in a
> classroom below the level of your own intelligence, being asked to do basic tasks you mastered
> long ago. Move the quiet, capable child early, challenge them, and give them a channel to be
> heard — and that percentage of behavior problems is never born at all.

---

## 1. Who this program is for

The child this program exists for: **nonverbal or semi-verbal, on the spectrum or with a
communication-channel disability, not a danger to themselves or others** — the child whose
barrier is *how they show what they know*, not behavior. Today that child is often placed by
default, and the clock matters: **every month in a room below their level is a month of
compounding frustration** — unheard, and re-drilled on tasks they already own. That frustration
is where a percentage of "behavior kids" are made. So the program's bias is **early**: identify
the quiet capable child fast, and get the placement conversation happening as soon as possible —
before the behaviors have a chance to form. (ILP makes the under-challenge itself visible: when a
child's mastery data shows a module was conquered long ago, being asked to repeat it stops
looking like instruction and starts looking like what it is.) This program gives their team a
real alternative, and gives every other ESE student the same meet-them-where-they-are machinery
along the way.

It is **not** a program to empty self-contained classrooms. Some children genuinely need that
setting, and their teams will keep choosing it. It is a program to make sure nobody lands there
**by default, for lack of evidence or lack of support in the mainstream.**

## 2. The five program commitments

1. **Same standard, adapted delivery.** The Learning Objective is locked; the channel, pace, and
   scaffolding adapt (the five axes). Rigor never silently drops.
2. **Channels never fade; scaffolds always do.** AAC, captions, speech-to-text are the child's
   voice and stay. Chunking, prompts, and models fade on evidence of independence.
3. **The IEP drives the software, automatically.** Accommodations ride on every assignment
   because the compiler carries them — never left to memory, never forgotten on test day.
4. **Same class, same app, same dignity.** No separate "special" software. The AAC student
   answers the same questions as their tablemates, through their channel.
5. **People decide; the platform proves.** Placement, eligibility, and any change to the
   standard belong to the IEP team and family. ILP's job is evidence.

## 3. The child's pathway through the program

**Step 1 — Find.** Universal K-entry baseline plus continuous classroom evidence catches the
never-screened and the misread (including the ASD/ADHD overlap). Signals route to people;
nothing is ever labeled by software.

**Step 2 — Understand.** The baseline meets the records: the IEP (or evaluation in progress) is
imported, its accommodations become forced adaptations, its goals decompose into module chains.
The child's profile now says *how they learn*, not what they "are."

**Step 3 — Place, with evidence on the table.** The IEP team meets with something no team has
had before: per-module mastery evidence **through the child's own channel**, the prompt-level
independence trend, and the documented safety record. The team and family decide — the platform
just makes sure the decision is informed. The parent app has told the family, in plain language,
what this meeting decides and what their seat at the table means.

**Step 4 — Support the mainstream day.** For the mainstreamed child:
- A **support facilitator / behavior tech** (per the IEP) covers transitions, lunch, and
  regulation — and sees the child's plan and today board in the app.
- In class, the child works the **same assignment as everyone**, auto-adapted: AAC response
  channel, visual-first, chunked steps — whatever their plan carries.
- **Pull-aside instruction is the module system**: the resource-room session works the *same*
  module chain as the classroom, so pull-out reinforces, and never becomes a parallel curriculum.
- Sensory and pacing supports (predictable structure, previewed transitions, breaks) ride along
  as plan-carried adaptations.

**Step 5 — Monitor honestly.** Every attempt is a timestamped data point on a module, tagged
with prompt level (independent → supported). Progress toward each IEP goal accrues as a
byproduct of instruction. The family sees growth in plain language between meetings — never a
quarterly surprise.

**Step 6 — Review and adjust.** At every IEP review the team sees: mastery by module, the
independence trend, scaffolds faded, supports delivered as the plan promised, and the safety
record. Placements get *defended by data* — in whichever direction the data points.

## 4. What a day looks like (one semi-verbal 7th-grader, mainstreamed)

Arrival: his support tech meets him at the bus loop; the transition plan is on her phone.
First period science: the class gets today's assignment; his version arrives with visual-first
presentation and his AAC vocabulary wired as the response channel — same questions, same rubric.
Mid-morning pull-aside: 25 minutes with the ESE teacher working the *same* fractions module his
class is on, one prompt level lighter. Lunch: supported transition, then peers. Afternoon: a
quiz — his accommodations applied automatically because the compiler carries his plan; his
answers through his device score into the same module tracking as everyone's. His mother checks
the parent app that evening and sees what he mastered, at what independence level. Nothing about
this day required his teacher to build special materials, and nothing about it happened in a
separate room except 25 intentional minutes.

## 5. Roles (and what ILP does for each)

| Role | Their job in the program | What ILP handles |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Gen-ed teacher** | Teach the class; assign once | Individualizing every assignment, carrying the plan, collecting the evidence |
| **ESE teacher / support facilitator** | Pull-aside instruction, coordination | The same module chain as the classroom; the child's plan and progress in one view |
| **Behavior tech / aide** | Transitions, regulation, lunch, safety | The day's plan and schedule; incident-free days accrue on the record |
| **SLP / OT / specialists** | Therapy goals, AAC vocabulary, co-design | Therapy goals as module chains; AAC symbol sets honored, not replaced |
| **ESE director** | Program integrity, compliance, staffing | Per-goal evidence, service-fidelity logs, and export to the IEP system of record |
| **The family** | The other half of every decision | Plain-language progress between meetings, and plain-language explanation of the process and their seat at the table |

## 6. How it rolls out (small, gated, credible)

- **Phase 0 — Co-design (before any child).** ESE teachers, SLPs, OTs, AAC specialists, and
  parents of ESE students shape the tools against this document. Nothing ships that the
  specialists haven't shaped. *(This phase is the program's credibility.)*
- **Phase 1 — Simulation.** The full pathway demonstrated end-to-end on synthetic students
  (much of it already runs — see §8) and refined with the co-design team.
- **Phase 2 — A pilot cohort.** A handful of consented families, one school, students whose
  teams independently support a mainstream placement. ILP runs alongside existing systems;
  nothing compliance-critical depends on it yet.
- **Phase 3 — Evaluate, then earn scale.** Publish the pilot's evidence — including to the
  families. Scale only what the evidence supports.

## 7. How the program measures itself

- **Academic:** modules mastered on grade-level objectives, through each child's channel.
- **Independence:** prompt-level trend; scaffolds faded and staying faded.
- **The prevention thesis (the headline research question):** behavior-incident rates for
  mainstreamed-with-support students vs. historical/self-contained baselines. No district has
  ever been able to measure this; this program can. Framed as a question the pilot answers, not
  a promise it makes.
- **Service fidelity:** supports delivered exactly as the IEP promised, on the record.
- **Family experience:** do parents report knowing where their child stands, and knowing their
  role in decisions?
- **Program integrity:** zero software-made placement/eligibility/modification decisions — by
  construction, verified in the audit trail.

## 8. Honest status — what exists today vs. what this program still needs

**Already built and demonstrable** (live at `/ese`): the IEP-as-live-input compiler (forced +
excluded accommodations), the AAC/alternate-response channel as full mastery evidence on the
same objectives, fade-vs-permanent support semantics, the module system that makes pull-aside
the same curriculum, the screening taxonomy with human-routed referral, and the family app with
acknowledge-to-clear communication.

**Still to build:** prompt-level capture in mastery records, the IEP import/export interfaces to
the district's system of record, the support-facilitator view, per-setting progress views, the
parent-facing plain-language process guide — and above all, **Phase 0 itself**: none of this is
pilot-ready until the specialists have shaped it. Costs the district must still own: the humans —
the behavior tech, the ESE staffing, the specialist time. The program makes those investments
work; it does not replace them. But the district doesn't carry them alone: local-college intern
pipelines and grant funding are mapped in [`staffing-pipeline.md`](staffing-pipeline.md) and
[`research-funding.md`](research-funding.md).

---

*The program in one line: find the child early, learn how they learn, put the evidence on the
table, support the mainstream day so it actually works, measure honestly, and let every decision
stay human — so that meeting a child where they are becomes the route to somewhere, for the kids
the current defaults quietly leave behind.*
