# The staffing pipeline: local colleges as the trained-hands supply

Trained staffing is always the issue — the behavior tech, the ESE support, the extra adult in
the inclusion classroom. Can we partner with FAU, Indian River State College, and Keiser
University to put Early Childhood Education majors and behavior-analyst trainees in classrooms
in exchange for intern hours? **Yes — and the machinery mostly already exists.** The currency
is real: teacher candidates must complete supervised field placements to graduate, and
BCBA candidates must log **1,500–2,000 supervised fieldwork hours** to sit for their exam.
Placement capacity is the universities' binding constraint; St. Lucie has the classrooms.
Compiled August 2026; items marked *confirm* need a phone call, not more research.

> **The headline finding: FAU already places student teachers in St. Lucie.** FAU's College of
> Education Office for School Engagement lists current relationships with Broward, Palm Beach,
> Indian River, **St. Lucie**, Martin, and Okeechobee districts, with a 15-week full-time
> student-teaching internship in those districts. The agreements, placement office, and clinical
> procedures already exist — the ask is *expansion into ESE and ABA placements*, not creation.
> And **FAU CARD (Center for Autism and Related Disabilities) has an office in Port St. Lucie**
> serving this exact county, providing free professional development to teachers.

## 1. What each institution actually offers

### FAU — the anchor partner
- **ESE teacher prep:** B.A./B.S. in Exceptional Student Education (K-12) leading to Florida
  ESE certification with reading and TESOL endorsements; Elementary Education bachelor's; a
  non-degree ESE certification track; an online M.Ed. in Special Education.
- **The ABA pipeline:** a **fully online M.Ed. in Special Education with an ABA concentration**
  (~20–24 months) whose coursework meets BCBA education requirements — but supervised fieldwork
  is a separate requirement students must find in the community. **That is the opening:** FAU
  ABA students need fieldwork sites; St. Lucie classrooms can be those sites. (*Confirm: the
  program's status under the BACB's new Pathway 2 attestation system that replaced the old
  verified-course-sequence system on 1/1/2026.*)
- **Existing St. Lucie placements** (above) and **FAU CARD Port St. Lucie** — free autism
  consultation and teacher/para PD, likely needing no board action at all.
- Note: FAU's physical Treasure Coast education campus closed in 2012; its presence here is the
  placement network + CARD, and the ABA master's is online — which suits working locals.

### Indian River State College — the local teacher-prep partner
- **B.S. in Exceptional Student Education** and **B.S. in Elementary Education** (both with
  reading and ESOL endorsements), associate pathways, and an **Educator Preparation Institute**
  fast-track for career-changers who already hold a bachelor's.
- Field experience is built into the programs: a minimum of three different placements
  including student teaching, each under a different cooperating teacher. (*Confirm: exact
  hour counts, and whether a current IRSC–St. Lucie placement agreement exists — near-certain
  given geography and the existing dual-enrollment partnership, but not directly documented.*)
- **No ABA/RBT program surfaced at IRSC** — an opportunity, not a gap: IRSC could host a
  district-cosponsored 40-hour RBT training (school-specific curricula for exactly this
  purpose are commercially available).
- **The grow-your-own opening:** Florida's PCOG-GYO Registered Teacher Apprenticeship funds
  paras with an AA to complete a tuition-free bachelor's while working under a mentor teacher.
  IRSC is absent from published recipient lists so far — **a joint St. Lucie + IRSC
  application is the obvious move.**

### Keiser University (Port St. Lucie campus)
- Its Treasure Coast campus is Port St. Lucie (no Fort Pierce campus). Value to the district:
  **M.S. in Behavioral Health** students (online/at Florida campuses; coursework described as
  meeting BCBA education requirements) who live locally and need fieldwork hours, plus B.A.
  Psychology students (ABA concentration exists at some campuses) needing experience. Not a
  teacher-prep pipeline; an MOU here would be new and smaller. (*Confirm: the Port St. Lucie
  campus's exact program list and the behavioral programs' current BCBA-pathway status.*)
- **Verified addition: Keiser runs an ACOTE-accredited A.S. in Occupational Therapy Assistant
  at multiple Florida campuses including Port St. Lucie** — two years, with required in-person
  clinical fieldwork, leading to national certification (NBCOT) and state licensure. OTA
  students need fieldwork placements; school-based practice is a recognized OTA setting. This
  is a direct local pipeline for hands behind the district's own occupational therapists.

## 2. The credential mechanics (why intern-hours-for-classroom-time works)

**RBT (Registered Behavior Technician)** — the inexpensive credential a district can sponsor
for its own paraprofessionals: 18+, high-school diploma, background check, a BACB-approved
**40-hour training**, a competency assessment, and an exam. Once certified, an RBT works only
under a BCBA's supervision (≥5% of service hours per month). RBTs already work in Florida
schools — Broward's ESE collaboration documents recognize them, and Pasco publishes RBT
supervision rules for school settings. St. Lucie already employs the very job classes this
fills: "Aides–Behavior Tech (FED-IDEA)," ESE paras, and support-facilitation teachers.

**BCBA fieldwork** — the big currency: **2,000 supervised hours** (≥5% supervised monthly) or
**1,500 concentrated hours** (≥10% monthly), with ≥60% of hours in "unrestricted"
analyst-level activities — functional behavior assessments, behavior-plan drafting, data
systems — which is exactly the analyst-level work the district needs done anyway. BACB rules
explicitly allow hours to accrue **through employment in any setting where behavior-analytic
services are delivered** — so a district-employed para enrolled in FAU's (or USF's online, or
Keiser's) program can log fieldwork *on the job*, if a qualified BCBA supervises. USF operates
a formal process for an agency to become an approved fieldwork site hosting master's students
at ~20 hours/week — the model to copy. (*Confirm: whether St. Lucie currently employs any
BCBAs; if not, supervision is contracted from the university side — a standard arrangement.*)

**Therapy-assistant pipelines — more hands behind the therapists the district already
employs.** The district's clinical core (SLPs, OTs) is in-house, and its behavior-tech service
is already contracted to outside agencies (three companies today, per district experience —
*confirm names and contract terms in discovery*). The "more hands on deck" play is
assistant-level extension of that core:
- **OTA (occupational therapy assistant):** Keiser Port St. Lucie's accredited program (above)
  requires clinical fieldwork; certified OTAs then work under supervising OTs — each district
  OT's caseload reach grows.
- **SLPA (speech-language pathology assistant):** **Florida licenses SLPAs**, with a licensed
  SLP permitted to supervise **up to two full-time (or three part-time) assistants** who screen,
  administer assessment tools and therapy activities, prepare materials, and document — under
  direct supervision (the SLP in the same facility). Every district SLP can double their hands
  without doubling their headcount, and SLPA candidates need supervised clinical experience.
  (*Confirm: which local programs feed SLPA licensure, and district SLP supervision capacity.*)
- **PTA (physical therapist assistant):** **IRSC runs an accredited A.S. PTA program** in Fort
  Pierce — the same pattern where an IEP includes physical therapy as a related service.

**Statewide context that strengthens the grant case:** the Florida Developmental Disabilities
Council's 2023 report documents the behavior-therapist shortage — 34 approved 40-hour training
providers statewide, only 4 university-affiliated. A district–university RBT/BCBA pipeline in
St. Lucie is the kind of gap-filler that report was written to provoke.

## 3. The legal checklist (all routine, all precedented)

1. **Level 2 background screening** (Jessica Lunsford Act; F.S. 1012.32/.465/.467): every
   non-employee with direct student contact clears FDLE/FBI fingerprint screening through the
   district; rescreening every 5 years. University affiliation agreements in Florida routinely
   build this in.
2. **Affiliation agreement / MOU**, board-approved. Ready templates exist in the public record:
   Lee County's signed MOUs with Grand Canyon and Baylor, Pasco's with Nova Southeastern.
   Typical terms: university carries liability (~$1M/occurrence, $3M aggregate), background
   checks, FERPA/confidentiality, supervision duties.
3. **Student-teacher legal status** (F.S. 1012.39): candidates jointly assigned by the program
   and school board, serving under a certified educator, get the same legal protections as
   certified educators.
4. **Clinical Educator Training** (F.S. 1004.04; Rule 6A-5.066): district teachers who
   supervise teacher-prep students must complete FLDOE Clinical Educator Training (K-3
   supervisors also need a reading credential). A district PD task, not a barrier.
5. **For ABA fieldwork:** a BACB-qualified supervisor designated in writing, with monthly
   documentation per BACB rules; mirror other districts' local supervision policies in the MOU.

## 4. Model programs to copy, and who pays

- **USF × Hillsborough Urban Teacher Residency** — the canonical Florida district–university
  co-designed residency.
- **UWF Para-to-Teacher Support Program (FLDOE-funded)** — pays tuition, textbooks, and exam
  costs for paras *currently working in Florida public schools* to earn **special-education
  degrees**. St. Lucie paras are eligible **today, at zero district cost.**
- **Florida PCOG-GYO teacher apprenticeship** — $15M state money to date, annual cycles; paras
  with an AA earn a tuition-free bachelor's while working. The St. Lucie + IRSC joint
  application above.
- **USF's ABA fieldwork-site affiliation** — the template for making St. Lucie an approved
  fieldwork site for FAU/USF-online/Keiser ABA students.
- **RBT training seats** — the district's behavior-tech job class is already IDEA-funded, and
  vendors note districts pay for school-RBT training seats from education grant funds. Pairs
  with the funding paths in [`research-funding.md`](research-funding.md).

## 5. The proposed structure (what to actually sign)

| Agreement | With | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Extend the existing FAU relationship** | FAU Office for School Engagement | A target number of ESE / support-facilitation placements, plus an **ABA fieldwork-site addendum** naming St. Lucie an approved site for FAU M.Ed.-ABA students, with designated BCBA supervision |
| **Clinical-placement MOU** | IRSC | ESE and Elementary Ed field placements prioritized into inclusion classrooms; joint **PCOG-GYO apprenticeship application** for district paras |
| **Practicum + fieldwork MOU** | Keiser Port St. Lucie | Psychology / M.S. Behavioral Health practicum students, plus **OTA clinical-fieldwork placements** under the district's supervising OTs |
| **Service relationship (likely no board action)** | FAU CARD Port St. Lucie | Free teacher/para professional development and autism consultation |

**Who does what in the classroom:**
- *Teacher-prep candidates (FAU/IRSC):* early field experiences → practica → the 15-week
  full-time internship, in inclusion and support-facilitation classrooms, under
  Clinical-Educator-trained cooperating ESE teachers.
- *ABA master's trainees:* ~20 hrs/week as behavior techs/aides for mainstreamed students,
  logging BACB fieldwork whose required analyst-level component (FBAs, behavior plans, data
  systems) delivers real value to the district while it counts toward their hours.
- *District paras (grow-your-own):* sponsored 40-hour RBT training → certified RBTs under
  district/university BCBA supervision → onward to BCaBA/BCBA (paid hours count as fieldwork)
  or to teaching degrees via Para-to-Teacher / PCOG-GYO.

**What each side gets.** Students and paras: required hours, free or cheap credentials,
tuition support, and hiring priority. The district: a continuous supply of screened,
supervised, trained adults in inclusion classrooms — the exact staffing the ESE program's
mainstream day depends on — plus a pre-vetted hiring pipeline. The colleges: guaranteed
placement capacity, their binding constraint. Nobody writes a big check.

## 6. How this connects to the rest of the program

The ESE program ([`ese-program.md`](ese-program.md)) is honest that the district must still
own the humans — the behavior tech, the ESE staffing, the specialist time. This pipeline is
how that cost gets structurally cheaper without getting thinner: intern hours are the one
labor supply that *renews itself every semester* and arrives pre-supervised. And the same
university partnership is the research partner the funding paths in
[`research-funding.md`](research-funding.md) require — one relationship, both problems.
