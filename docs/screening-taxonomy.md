# Universal screening taxonomy — the gateway into ESE

The research-backed model of processing domains and observable indicators an ILP universal
screener watches for, so the platform can **(a) catch early signals, (b) adapt curriculum and
delivery, and (c) route to a human** for possible formal evaluation and IEP eligibility.

> **Non-negotiable premise: this SCREENS, it does not DIAGNOSE.** A screener estimates elevated
> likelihood/risk and produces a *referral signal*; it never assigns a condition label. This mirrors
> the field's own validated screeners — the M-CHAT-R/F is described as assessing "the likelihood for
> autism," where an elevated score means the child "should be referred for diagnostic evaluation."
> Even a high-sensitivity screener has sub-50% positive predictive value, which is exactly why an
> elevated result means *refer*, not *has the condition*. Eligibility is decided by a team of
> qualified professionals **and the parents**, after informed written consent — never by an algorithm.

Two structural facts drive the design:
- In Florida, special education is **Exceptional Student Education (ESE)** — the 13 federal IDEA
  categories, plus **Gifted** under the ESE umbrella.
- The correct pre-referral engine is **MTSS/RTI** (a tiered, preventive support framework), not a gate
  to special ed. Florida permits **both** the RtI approach and the discrepancy model for SLD.

**Condition ≠ eligibility category.** Several high-signal conditions (dyslexia, dysgraphia,
dyscalculia, ADHD, CAPD, VPD, DCD/dyspraxia, NVLD, anxiety, giftedness) are **not standalone IDEA
categories** — they surface *through* categories such as Specific Learning Disability, Other Health
Impairment, Speech/Language Impairment, Emotional Disturbance, Autism, or the sensory categories, or
through Section 504 / Gifted. The engine models observable **domain-indicator patterns**; only humans
populate the eligibility category.

## Conditions the screener is prepared to flag

Each is modeled as a pattern across processing domains, with early indicators by age band
(PreK-K · 1-3 · 4-8 · 9-12), a referral pathway, the specialists involved, and how instruction adjusts.
Full per-condition detail (indicators, evaluators, adjustments) lives in the research record; summary:

| Condition | Core domains flagged | Not an IDEA category on its own? | Typical evaluators |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Dyslexia** (reading SLD) | phonological awareness, rapid naming, verbal working memory, orthographic | via SLD | school psych, reading specialist, SLP |
| **Dysgraphia** (writing SLD) | fine/visual-motor, orthographic, written expression | via SLD | school psych, OT |
| **Dyscalculia** (math SLD) | number sense, math reasoning, working memory, retrieval | via SLD | school psych, math interventionist |
| **ADHD** + executive function | attention, working memory, inhibition, flexibility, processing speed | via OHI / 504 (medical dx by provider) | school team + pediatrician/psychiatrist |
| **Autism spectrum** | social communication, sensory, executive function, flexibility | IDEA category | dev. pediatrician, psychologist, SLP, OT |
| **Speech/Language impairment** | receptive/expressive language, articulation | IDEA category | SLP (lead), audiologist |
| **Auditory processing (CAPD)** | auditory processing, auditory working memory | domain indicator | audiologist, SLP |

## The expanded screening-domain taxonomy (what the engine tracks)

The engine models a **signal per processing domain**, never a diagnosis. 25 domains + observable
indicator types, each implicating one or more conditions:

1. Phonological awareness · 2. Rapid automatized naming · 3. Orthographic processing/spelling ·
4. Oral language — receptive · 5. Oral language — expressive · 6. Articulation/speech ·
7. Reading fluency/decoding · 8. Reading comprehension (inferential) · 9. Number sense/math reasoning ·
10. Working memory · 11. Processing speed · 12. Attention (sustained/selective) · 13. Executive function ·
14. Auditory processing · 15. Visual processing · 16. Visual-spatial reasoning ·
17. Fine motor / visual-motor · 18. Gross motor / motor planning · 19. Social communication/pragmatics ·
20. Sensory — auditory acuity · 21. Sensory — visual acuity · 22. Sensory processing/regulation ·
23. Emotional / self-regulation · 24. Adaptive / general cognitive · 25. Advanced-ability/acceleration.

### Four cross-cutting filters (mandatory modifiers on every signal — the defense against bias)

1. **ELL / L1 filter** — is the difficulty present in the home language too? Only-in-English ⇒ likely
   acquisition, not disability. Cross-linguistic ⇒ possible disability.
2. **Instructional-adequacy filter** — has the student had adequate, appropriate instruction/opportunity?
   (RTI's core question.)
3. **Sensory-first filter** — rule out uncorrected hearing/vision loss before attributing to a
   processing disorder (sensory loss masquerades as inattention/language problems).
4. **2e co-occurrence flag** — a high-ability signal + any deficit signal triggers a twice-exceptional
   review; never let strong overall performance suppress a disability referral.

## Signal-strength → next-step routing (the action ladder)

Nothing here diagnoses; every rung is a *support* or a *hand-off to a human*.

| Signal | Meaning | Automated next step | Human step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **0 · Watch** | isolated/occasional indicator | universal monitoring; light differentiation | none |
| **1 · Emerging** (Tier 1/2) | pattern below benchmark | adapt delivery, targeted practice; **begin progress monitoring** | teacher notified; log in MTSS |
| **2 · Persistent** (Tier 3) | inadequate response over time | intensify; compile evidence packet | teacher + interventionist + **family notification** |
| **3 · Referral threshold** | cross-domain, durable, adverse impact after intervention | **stop escalating autonomously**; assemble data | team reviews; **recommend formal evaluation**; obtain **written parental consent** |
| **Sensitive-flag override** | social-communication cluster, sensory, or emotional/safety | route immediately regardless of tier | family + specialist/medical referral; heightened privacy |

## The gateway into ESE

When accumulated, progress-monitored signals cross the referral threshold, the platform **assembles
evidence and hands off** — it never decides:

1. **MTSS/RTI is the pre-referral framework** — documented inadequate response to intensive,
   evidence-based intervention is what justifies moving toward evaluation.
2. **Human-decided referral** — a qualified team (with the family) reviews the data and *recommends* a
   formal evaluation; the platform outputs a referral recommendation with supporting evidence, phrased
   as risk/evidence, never a diagnosis or category.
3. **Informed written parental consent is mandatory** before any initial evaluation; prior written
   notice is required.
4. **Comprehensive evaluation → eligibility staffing** — a multidisciplinary team + parents determine
   ESE eligibility and category.
5. **IEP (or 504)** — if eligible; for the most significant cognitive disabilities the IEP team may
   designate alternate standards/assessment. See [`ese-iep-track.md`](ese-iep-track.md).

**The screener is the gateway: it catches early, supports in general education, escalates on data, and —
only when humans agree — opens the door to evaluation. It never walks through that door itself.**

## Guardrails

1. **Screen, never diagnose.** Every output is a likelihood/risk signal that routes to a person.
2. **Bias & over-identification** — apply the ELL/L1 filter; audit the platform's *own* referral rates
   by subgroup. IDEA monitors **significant disproportionality** (a risk ratio > 3.00 for three years
   triggers corrective action + 15% CCEIS set-aside). Mirror equity pathways like Florida's **Plan B**
   for underrepresented gifted students. The **1.0% alternate-assessment cap** and the **3.00 risk-ratio**
   are concrete numeric guardrails worth encoding as monitoring metrics.
3. **MTSS/RTI first** — refer on documented inadequate response, not a single low score.
4. **FERPA / IDEA privacy & consent** — screening data are education records; limit access, honor the
   right to inspect, require written consent before evaluation, handle emotional/behavioral and
   mental-health flags with heightened privacy care.
5. **Accommodation vs. modification discipline** — accommodations keep the standard; modifications /
   alternate standards change it and apply narrowly (most significant cognitive disabilities).
6. **Human-in-the-loop is absolute** — no referral, diagnosis, or eligibility decision is automated.
   The engine's ceiling is: adapt instruction, monitor, and *recommend a human review*.
