ILP · A briefing for district leadership

The test tells you a child is behind.
Then what?

St. Lucie already spends on tools that measure students three times a year — FAST and i-Ready. They are good at measuring. The question this paper answers is what happens in the weeks between the tests, and why that gap is where ILP makes the standalone diagnostic obsolete.

First, in fairness

What these tools actually do

A credible case starts by describing them accurately — not knocking down a straw man.

FAST — the state's yardstick

The Florida Assessment of Student Thinking replaced the old FSA in 2022-23. It runs three times a year (PM1 fall, PM2 winter, PM3 spring); PM3 is the summative score that feeds school grades. Grades 3–10 take computer-adaptive ELA and math from Cambium; K–2 use Star.1 It is required by the state — it is how accountability works, and it is not going away.

i-Ready — the district's diagnostic

St. Lucie administers the i-Ready Diagnostic three times a year to identify needs and generate personalized-practice pathways.2 Public list pricing runs about $6–$30 per student, per subject, per year.3 It is a genuinely useful point-in-time picture — and a separate tool students stop and log into.

Both are measurement instruments. Neither teaches the lesson, adapts it to the child, builds the reteach, or takes a single task off the teacher's plate. That division of labor is the whole point of what follows.

The shared blind spot

Three snapshots, and a lot of dark between them

Both tools sample the year three times. Instruction happens continuously. So a student who slips in week 3 is often invisible until the winter window in week 18 — and in third grade, that lag has a legal cost (more on that below).

The same school year, measured two ways.

ILP doesn't add a fourth test. It reads the work students already do — every assigned objective, item and submission is evidence, scored against the same B.E.S.T. standards FAST reports on. The picture is always current because it's a byproduct of teaching, not an interruption of it.

The honest verdict

What becomes obsolete — and what doesn't

Overclaiming would be easy and wrong. Here is the precise version, which is also the more persuasive one.

Becomes redundant

The standalone diagnostic (i-Ready's PM role)

Once mastery is tracked continuously against B.E.S.T. from real classwork, a separate tool that stops instruction three times a year to tell you the same thing — later — is doing work ILP already did all semester.

That's a line item, and staff-and-student hours, the district can reconsider once ILP is proven.

Stays — and students walk in ready

FAST (the state test)

FAST is required by law; ILP neither removes nor replaces it, and we won't pretend otherwise. ILP's job is to get students ready to take it.

Because students spend the year mastering the exact B.E.S.T. standards FAST measures — with gaps caught and retaught as they appear — they arrive at each window better prepared, and the score confirms learning that already happened instead of revealing a surprise too late to fix.

The category that dies is "bolt-on measurement." The bar chart three times a year, disconnected from the next day's lesson, is what ILP makes obsolete. The state test remains — ILP's job is to send students in prepared for it.
Why this matters in St. Lucie, specifically

The cost of finding out in week 18

St. Lucie just earned its first-ever "A" (2025), climbing from a B.4 This isn't a rescue argument — it's a how-do-we-hold-and-extend-an-A argument. And the clearest pressure point is third-grade reading.

≈49%
St. Lucie grade-3 ELA at Level 3+ (2024) — below the state's ≈53–55%5
+4 pts
Year-over-year gain in grade-3 ELA — real momentum to protect5
Level 2
Score below it on the grade-3 ELA test and a child faces mandatory retention6

Under Florida F.S. 1008.25, a third grader who scores below Level 2 must be retained absent a good-cause exemption.6 When the first clear signal arrives at the winter or spring window, the runway to intervene before that stakes-bearing spring test is already short. A model that surfaces the same risk in week 3 — and opens the reteach automatically — is worth more here than one more accurate snapshot in week 18.

Grade 3 is also its own line in the state formula. Florida scores Grade-3 ELA as a separate school-grade component, alongside learning gains and the gains of the lowest 25%.7 Those are exactly the students a continuous, remediation-first model reaches first.

The bottom line

Stop paying to be told. Start getting students ready.

The district is already paying — in dollars and in instructional days — to measure students. ILP proposes to spend the district's effort on acting on what those measures show, continuously, with the teacher in command, so that students are prepared long before the state test. Keep the FAST test it's legally bound to; retire the redundant standalone diagnostic once ILP proves the loop; and give third-grade teachers the weeks back that the three-window model quietly costs them.

ILP doesn't replace the state test. It sends students in ready for it.
