St. Lucie runs IXL for practice, Skyward for grades, and Canvas for assignments — three disconnected tools, with teachers as the glue. ILP is the one integrated system that does all of it, and does more.

The shift

Stop stitching. Start with one.

Every tool in the stack does one slice. None of them talk to each other, so the teacher carries the load of connecting them — and the student and parent see a fragmented picture. ILP replaces the seams with a single connected system.

What none of the three can do

More than integrated — a level up

◇

One place, one flow

In-class work, homework, and individually tailored study guides — assigned once, individualized for every student, all connected to the grade and the reteach.

◎

Meets every learner where they are

A baseline for every student, not a bolt-on diagnostic — so instruction starts from the truth about each child and strengthens the weak spots over time.

↻

Fixes it the same day

Miss an objective and remediation opens immediately — a materially different reteach, then equivalent reassessment. No dead-end grade, no waiting for the next test window.

◆

Collaboration worth their screen time

A safe, verified space — even across schools — where students work together on projects that demand critical thinking and problem-solving. It pulls kids off garbage social media and points that same energy at learning. Moderated, no DMs, no follower games.

▷

Scenarios are assignments, not games

Branching, scenario-based simulations aren't optional practice — they're real, graded assignments that produce evidence of decision-making and reasoning.

✦

Teacher in command · parents back in

The teacher assigns, reviews, and releases every grade. Parents get a real-time, plain-language window into effort and progress — brought back into the mix, not locked out.

The through-line: the old stack measures and stores. ILP teaches, connects, and includes — students to each other, parents to the classroom, every objective to the reteach that follows it.
How you get there

A pilot, not a leap of faith

You don't swap a district's whole system overnight, and we won't pretend you should. ILP is new — a working system, not yet a proven one — so it earns its place the honest way: one grade, real classrooms, teachers in control, measured against real outcomes.

But the pilot isn't an add-on bolted next to IXL. It's the first grade of the transition — the beginning of replacing the reasons the district needed three separate tools in the first place. When it works, it doesn't sit beside your stack. It becomes it.

The old model — on paper or on a screen — hands out a worksheet, drills it, grades it, moves on. Digitizing that model didn't reinvent teaching. It just lit the worksheet up.

We're not a better worksheet.
We're what replaces it.

A living system where students learn by doing, solve real problems together, and are met where they are — moved forward with evidence, with the teacher leading and parents finally in the room. On the standards St. Lucie already uses.
