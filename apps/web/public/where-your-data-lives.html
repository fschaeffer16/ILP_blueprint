ILP · A briefing for district IT & leadership

Your data never
leaves your control.

The first question a district asks a new platform is “where does our students’ data live?” Here is the straight answer — the hosting options, the privacy guardrails that apply the moment real data is involved, and an honest line on what is and isn’t real in today’s demo.

The short answer

“The district’s server” means a server the district controls — not necessarily one it racks and cools

There are three ways to run ILP for real. In every one, the student data is legally the district’s, governed by a signed data-privacy agreement, and never sold or repurposed. They differ only in who operates the machines.

Most common

District cloud tenant

ILP runs in a cloud account (AWS, Azure, or Google Cloud) provisioned for the district — their tenant, their encryption keys, their region. This is how Canvas, Skyward, and IXL themselves largely run.

Runs on: district’s cloud · Data owner: the district
Maximum control

District data center (on-prem)

For districts whose policy requires student data on hardware they physically own, ILP installs there. It’s a standard TypeScript/Node app with a normal database — no exotic dependencies — so their IT team can host it.

Runs on: district hardware · Data owner: the district
Fastest to pilot

Vendor-hosted subscription

ILP hosts it and the district subscribes — exactly the model they already use for IXL. The data is still theirs by contract; the servers are ours. Lowest lift to get a pilot running.

Runs on: ILP’s cloud · Data owner: the district (by contract)

So the instinct “that would have to be on the district’s server” is right in spirit: the real, live version runs on infrastructure the district owns or controls — whichever of the three fits their policy.

Honest status

What’s live today is a demo on synthetic data

The working system at ilp-blueprint-web.vercel.app holds no real student information. Every name, score, and message is invented for the demo. That is deliberate — and it’s why the parent-notification receipt loop is presenter-controlled and lives only in your browser right now: there is intentionally no backend holding real data yet.

The line that matters. The moment ILP touches a real child’s grade or a real parent’s phone, that data must live under the district’s data-governance umbrella. Nothing about the demo shortcuts that — it’s a gate to pass, on purpose.
The guardrails

What applies the moment the data is real

These aren’t features to bolt on later — they’re conditions ILP is designed to meet before a single real student is enrolled.

RequirementWhat it means in ILP
FERPAEducation records stay under district control; access is role-scoped and logged; no third-party resale. ILP is the processor, the district is the owner.
Florida student-data privacyAligns with Florida’s student-data-privacy requirements and the district’s existing data-sharing agreements — the same framework governing i-Ready and IXL today.
Encryption & accessEncrypted in transit and at rest; least-privilege access; a full audit trail (the engine already writes an append-only log of grade decisions).
Data residencyRuns in the region and tenant the district specifies — their cloud or their hardware.
Retention & deletionRetention windows and deletion-on-request follow district policy, not the vendor’s convenience.
The privacy line, in codeParents see patterns, time, growth, and safety — never their child’s or other children’s messages. There is deliberately no message-content field in the parent data model.

Before real students — the pilot gates, in order

  1. Technical feasibility on synthetic data with teacher review — done
  2. Content & assessment review by subject experts and psychometric advisors
  3. Privacy, security & district legal approval — this page’s subject
  4. Small classroom usability pilot without high-stakes grading
  5. Controlled instructional pilot with predefined outcome measures
  6. Independent evaluation before any broader expansion
Why this is a strength, not a hurdle

Owning your data beats renting a black box

A district can’t promise a parent much about what a vendor does with their child’s data inside a rented platform. With ILP, the answer is simple and true: your data stays under your control, and the content is yours. That’s a promise a black box can’t make — and it’s a reason a district feels safer choosing this path, not warier.

The question isn’t whether we’ll protect your data. It’s that it was never ours to begin with.
