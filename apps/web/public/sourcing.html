How the curriculum is built

Learning Objectives come from standards. Content comes from vetted, current sources.

Nothing a child sees is invented free-hand or pulled from an unvetted corner of the web. Learning targets are mapped from official standards; teaching materials are drawn from a governed library of credible, openly-licensed or licensed sources — each with provenance, a license, and a review date. The teaching techniques are evidence-based cognitive science from the last two decades, not mid-century rote.

01  Where Learning Objectives come from

Standards bodies define the target; ILP versions and governs it.

An Learning Objective is never a teacher’s guess about what matters. It is authored from an official standards framework, reviewed by a curriculum specialist, then published as a versioned, immutable record. That chain is what makes the Learning Objective auditable and reusable across every classroom.

02  Where the content is mined from

A tiered, licensed library — provenance on every item.

Content is drawn from five tiers, strongest first. Each source carries a license and a review status before anything can reach a student. The system prefers primary sources and openly-licensed materials; district-licensed publisher content is used only where the contract permits.

03  How a source becomes usable

The vetting pipeline.

No source reaches a student on discovery. Each passes a fixed gate and is stored as a record with its citation, license, authority and review date — then re-checked on a schedule. This is the editorial governance the blueprint requires, made concrete.

Editorial standard (built into every source record)

Statements are marked as documented fact, inference, interpretation, or disputed. Sources, revision history and editorial decisions are exposed to reviewers. Verified errors are corrected immediately with the audit trail preserved. The platform teaches the full documented record — it does not advocate a contemporary partisan or identity position.

04  Teaching techniques — evidence-based, not mid-century

How lessons are built and adapted.

Every adaptation the compiler can apply is grounded in a named, modern body of research. These are the methods that replace one-size worksheets, rote recall and fixed ability tracking.

TechniqueOriginHow ILP uses it
Rosenshine’s Principles of Instruction2012 · cognitive scienceSmall steps, guided practice, high success rate → the guided-practice and worked-example-with-fading patterns.
Universal Design for Learning (UDL) 3.0CAST · July 2024Multiple means of representation, engagement & action → read-aloud, visual-first, and choice of response method — access without changing the target.
Worked-example effect & fadingcognitive-load researchA fully worked parallel example, steps removed across practice → the worked-example-fade adaptation, faded before the mastery task.
Retrieval & spaced practiceDunlosky et al. 2013Spacing and low-stakes recall → practice scheduling and delayed retention checks in the growth model.
Formative assessment (assessment for learning)Black & WiliamEvidence used to adjust teaching → the entire teacher-reviewed grading + remediation loop.
Concrete–Representational–Abstract (CRA)math methods researchModels before symbols → the visual-first fraction adaptation.
Structured literacy / science of readingScarborough’s Reading RopeDecoding + language comprehension → vocabulary preview and read-aloud supports that never mask the reading Learning Objective itself.
Feedback that moves learning forwardHattie; WiliamCriterion-level evidence and specific next steps → the grading rubric output and reflection prompts.

✕ What ILP is not (the 1950s model)

  • One worksheet for thirty different children
  • Rote memorization as the goal, not a foundation
  • Fixed ability tracks and permanent labels
  • A textbook chapter as the whole curriculum
  • Grades with no evidence and no next step

✓ What ILP uses instead

  • One Learning Objective → an individualized version per child
  • Knowledge in service of reasoning and transfer
  • Temporary, evidence-based groups that dissolve
  • Primary sources and current, licensed materials
  • Evidence, teacher judgment, and mandatory remediation
