A partnership proposal · ILP

Built by teachers.
Owned by the people who use it.

Most classroom technology is designed for teachers by people who left the classroom — or never entered it. ILP is the other way around. This is an invitation for St. Lucie County to help build it, and to grow with it.

For: Superintendent & School Board · Curriculum & Assessment leadership · From: the ILP founding team
01 The premise

The experts have had their turn

For thirty years, instructional software has arrived the same way: a vendor or a committee of specialists decides what teaching should look like, packages it, and ships it down into classrooms. Teachers receive it. They adapt around its gaps on their own time. When it doesn't fit a real child in a real room, that's treated as the teacher's problem to absorb.

It is worth saying plainly: the people who know the most about teaching a third grader to understand a fraction are the people who teach third graders, and the administrators who watch that process across dozens of classrooms every day. Not a distant curriculum author. Not an AI trained to sound confident.

ILP is built from the ground up by the people who do the work — and governed by the district that stands behind it.
02 The inversion

Who holds the pen

The difference isn't a feature. It's who authors the content and who the software answers to. In ILP the "expert layer" is inverted: the engine's only job is to enforce the guardrails a district sets — it never decides what is taught or waters down rigor to seem helpful.

The usual model

  • A vendor defines the pedagogy; teachers conform to it
  • Content is a black box you rent and can't shape
  • AI answers the student directly — and gives away the answer
  • Your data trains someone else's product
  • When it grows, the vendor captures the value

The ILP model

  • Teachers author Learning Objectives & lessons; the district approves them
  • Every source is vetted, licensed and visible — a living curriculum you own
  • AI coaches the thinking; the graded answer stays protected
  • Synthetic data first; student data never leaves district control
  • When it grows, the districts that built it share in that

Every one of those "ILP model" rows is already written into the working engine as a tested rule — not a promise on a slide.

03 Why it compounds

The part that grows exponentially

A textbook is finished the day it prints. ILP does the opposite — it gets deeper every time a teacher uses it, because the work teachers already do becomes shared, reusable, guardrail-checked content. That is a flywheel, and flywheels don't grow in a straight line.

Each turn adds vetted content, which meets more kids, which draws more teachers — who add more content.

One grade-3 teacher authoring one fractions Learning Objective isn't a rounding error — it's a permanent addition to a shared shelf every other grade-3 teacher can adapt. Multiply by a school, a district, a state that all teach to the same Florida B.E.S.T. standards, and the library compounds far faster than any single team could ever write it. And because every addition passes the same gates, depth never comes at the cost of rigor — the failure mode of open lesson marketplaces.

04 The path out

From one classroom to a national standard

Florida is the ideal launch point, not the ceiling. Because B.E.S.T. is a statewide standard, content built for St. Lucie ports directly to every other Florida district — and the same Learning Objective-mapping approach reaches any state's standards after that.

05 Why growth stays safe

The guardrails are the growth engine

Scale usually means drift — quality slips as content piles up. ILP is built so the opposite is true: the same rules that protect a single child are what let the library grow without losing credibility. They travel with every piece of content, into every new district.

Teacher authorityAI recommends; teachers decide and hold the grade.
Learning Objective integrityEvery lesson, item and fix traces to a standard. Rigor is visible.
Vetted sources onlyNothing unapproved ever reaches a student.
Answer-protected helpThe bot coaches thinking; it never hands over the answer.
Privacy by designNo hidden data model; parents see patterns, never private chats.
No manipulationRewards helpfulness and accuracy, not outrage or follower counts.
