Staffing it and funding it: the two questions that answer each other

Can local colleges supply trained hands in exchange for intern hours? Can grant money pay for trying teaching techniques and recording the data? Both answers are yes — and the same university partnership unlocks both. Research digest, August 2026.

The headline

The machinery mostly already exists

FAU already places student teachers in St. Lucie. FAU's College of Education lists a current placement relationship with the St. Lucie district (among six), including the 15-week full-time student-teaching internship. And FAU CARD — its Center for Autism and Related Disabilities — has an office in Port St. Lucie serving this exact county, with free professional development for teachers. The partnership ask is expansion into ESE and behavior-analyst placements, not creation from scratch.
Intern hours are the one labor supply that renews itself every semester — and arrives pre-supervised.
Question 1 · Staffing

The currency is real: hours students must log anyway

  • Teacher candidates (FAU ESE & Elementary Ed; IRSC's B.S. in Exceptional Student Education and Elementary Ed, right in Fort Pierce) must complete supervised field placements and a full-time internship to graduate. Florida law even gives properly assigned student teachers the same legal protections as certified educators.
  • Behavior-analyst trainees must log 1,500–2,000 supervised fieldwork hours to sit for the BCBA exam — and certification rules explicitly allow those hours to accrue in schools, including on the job for district-employed paras enrolled in a program. FAU runs a fully online ABA master's whose students need exactly these community fieldwork sites; Keiser's Port St. Lucie campus adds behavioral-health master's and psychology students needing practicum hours.
  • Grow-your-own for district paras: a sponsored 40-hour training makes a para a certified RBT (registered behavior technician — already a recognized role in Florida schools); state programs (UWF Para-to-Teacher, the PCOG-GYO apprenticeship) pay tuition for working paras to become ESE teachers. IRSC isn't on the apprenticeship recipient list yet — a joint district + IRSC application is the obvious move.
The legal checklist is routine and precedented: Level 2 fingerprint screening for every placed student (Jessica Lunsford Act), a board-approved affiliation agreement (Lee and Pasco counties' signed MOUs are public templates), Clinical Educator Training for the cooperating teachers, and a designated BCBA supervisor for the behavior-analyst trainees. No new law needed anywhere.
Why this matters most for ESE. General education has a fixed structure to adapt; ESE has far less — each child’s plan is effectively its own curriculum of supports, and what a child needs is often a person: a behavior tech for transitions, an AAC-fluent specialist, a second trained adult in the inclusion room. For an ESE child, “meet them where they are” includes who they need beside them — so the outside bench isn’t background logistics, it’s part of the individualization itself, and the program treats it as a first-class resource: when a plan calls for a support, the program knows which outside entity supplies it and how it gets paid for.

What each side gets

Students and paras get required hours, free or cheap credentials, and hiring priority. The district gets a continuous supply of screened, supervised, trained adults in inclusion classrooms — the exact staffing the ESE program's mainstream day depends on. The colleges get guaranteed placement capacity, their binding constraint. Nobody writes a big check.

Question 2 · Funding

Yes — and the pilot is shaped like what funders pay for

The program's own research question — does early supported inclusion prevent a percentage of acquired behavior problems? — is a genuine, unanswered question, and the platform is the data-collection instrument no prior study has had.

Ranked pathWhat it is
1ED/IES SBIR Federal money paid directly to the ed-tech company: $250K to add/validate the ESE module, then $1M over 2 years for a full school-based pilot evaluation — the district is the site, not the applicant. Operating now; annual spring cycle.
2Spencer Foundation RPP grant Up to $400K / 3 years, purpose-built for a district–university partnership studying a locally defined problem. Our question is a textbook fit. Annual cycle.
3Federal special-ed research (IES/NCSER) The highest ceiling (~$2M–$3.8M) and the best scientific home — but the agency is only now restarting after 2025 cuts; watch the fall 2026 announcements. Hedge: the same design fits NIH's behavior/communication lanes.
4Fund year one now: OAR + local Organization for Autism Research applied-research grants ($50K, autism-specific, fast) + the Children's Services Council of St. Lucie County — a local taxing district that funds school-success programs in this exact county and demands the outcome measurement this pilot is built around.
5Second wave OSEP "Stepping-Up" technology implementation (~$500K/yr) once the pilot has evidence; NSF's K-12 program if framed on adaptive STEM learning with AI.
And two no-competition bridges let the district start immediately: ESSA's Tier-4 evidence flexibility lets existing formula funds pay for an intervention precisely by committing to evaluate it — the evaluation is what ILP does natively — and IDEA lets up to 15% of the district's allocation fund early supports for not-yet-identified students in general education: a direct statutory hook for prevention.
Why they interlock

One partnership, both problems

The winning grant structure is a triad: the platform + a university partner + the district. The university supplies the principal investigator, the research oversight (IRB), and — through the placement pipeline above — the trained hands in classrooms. The district supplies the site and the question. The platform supplies the delivery and the data. So the ask to a superintendent is not "buy software." It is: join a district + university + platform pilot that outside money wants to fund, that can begin under flexibility you already have, and that answers a question no district has ever been able to measure.
